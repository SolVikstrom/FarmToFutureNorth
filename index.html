Farm to Future North

Four people a local food producer has to reach. Read one story from the person downward: what they want, what moves them, and what gets in the way.

01 · Store customer

In my role as a consumer, I want to buy food and ingredients, so that I can meet my own and my loved ones’ need for food.

Motivation

Habit and routine

People return to products they already like. New recipes arrive through cooks they trust, food apps, and cooking shows.

How might we

introduce a new brand, or a new food, into a habit that already feels settled?

Motivation

Convenience

They want everything in one place, in the shop or online, with as little extra time and logistics as possible.

How might we

make local food the easy, one-stop choice rather than a separate errand?

Motivation

Preferences and values

Diet, health, and the climate matter. So does a feeling of belonging with the place the food comes from.

How might we

create incentives to support local food that feel as natural as the habit itself?

Threshold

Sensory quality

They need to see and feel fresh food: the look, the smell, and whether it will last at home.

How might we

let people judge freshness before they have to trust a label?

Threshold

Budget

The food has to fit the household food budget. Value is judged at the moment of purchase.

How might we

adjust the price so it feels attractive, while the producer still earns a living?

02 · Store

In my role as a store operator, I want to buy and display food, so that I can sell it with a good profit per metre of shelf.

Threshold

Standardisation

The chain expects GS1 barcodes and pack sizes that fit the shelf. A beautiful product that does not fit does not get a place.

How might we

meet the shelf rules without erasing the local story?

Threshold

Logistics

Goods should arrive through the central warehouse, or as an approved direct delivery. Anything else is extra work.

How might we

enter the chain’s logistics and still keep the local story intact?

Threshold

Returns and terms

Unsold goods, campaign prices, and a purchase price that stays steady through the season all have to be agreed in advance.

How might we

write terms for unsold food that a small producer can actually live with?

Motivation

Turnover

The product has to leave the shelf before it becomes waste. A slow jar costs more than an empty space.

How might we

help local food leave the shelf before it spoils?

Threshold

Margin

The retail margin has to pay for the shelf. If the numbers do not work per metre, the product does not stay.

How might we

earn the shelf when the margin has to carry the space?

03 · Municipality

In my role as a meal service, I want to buy food, so that I can serve nutritious meals every day.

School kitchens, health care, and social care.

Threshold

Rules and procurement

Specifications, framework agreements, and certification decide who is allowed to sell. The process is strict, and hard for a small producer to enter.

How might we

pass public procurement rules without shutting out small producers?

Threshold

Delivery reliability

Meals leave at fixed times. The cold chain cannot break, and an empty order is not an option.

How might we

promise the clock, the cold chain, and a full delivery?

Threshold

Price per portion

There is a fixed maximum cost per portion. A fair price for the producer still has to fit inside that number.

How might we

fit a fair producer price inside a fixed cost per portion?

Motivation

Administrative ease

Few invoices and one combined delivery are easier to live with than many small suppliers arriving separately.

How might we

make one hub as easy to use as one wholesaler?

Motivation

Volume and scale

Several kitchens together can carry a steady order through the term, even when one kitchen’s need shifts.

How might we

let several kitchens share one steady order?

04 · Tourist

In my role as a tourist, I want to experience authentic local flavours and food culture, so that I can connect with the place and enjoy something I cannot find at home.

Motivation

Authenticity

Local ingredients, traditions, and the people behind the food are the reason to stop. The product alone is not the whole draw.

How might we

highlight the personal stories of producers?

Motivation

Arctic identity

Pristine nature, the seasons, wild ingredients, and a northern way of life are what make the food feel of this place.

How might we

make Arctic origin visible and easy to recognise?

Motivation

Souvenirs and memories

A jar or a bite can be a piece of the trip to take home, and to talk about later.

How might we

make local food an attractive souvenir?

Threshold

Easy to find

The food has to be where tourists already are: hotels, restaurants, attractions, airports, and online.

How might we

make Arctic food easy for tourists to discover?

Threshold

Value must be obvious

Visitors will pay more for a memorable experience, but only when the origin and the story are easy to understand.

How might we

make the extra price feel obvious, in a language a visitor can read?
